Global Research Governance / Participant Privacy Rules

The standing limits on what intervu.quest is allowed to do. Participant transcript non-exclusion, anonymity, and privacy are hard constraints that every technical, analytical, and operational choice must satisfy.

StatusIn force
VersionGRG-PPR-1.2.0
Effective dateSeptember 1, 2026
ScopeEntire intervu.quest platform

Purpose and authority

This page is the platform-wide governance and participant-privacy specification. It is intentionally separate from the ordinary researcher dashboard, interview protocol, global analysis rules, and project-specific analysis rules.

The dashboard shows what the system is currently doing. This page states what the system is permitted and prohibited from doing. Reviewers should assess implementation evidence against these rules; the existence of a rule is not, by itself, a claim of compliance.

Hard constraint

No participant transcript may be administratively excluded or rendered non-processible, and participants remain anonymous at all times. AI and system outcomes have no authority to disqualify a contribution. These protections cannot be traded away for database convenience, easier authentication, analytical automation, or operational speed.

Standing platform-wide rules

“Must” and “must not” are binding platform requirements. Each stable rule ID is retained across versions so changes can be traced without silently rewriting the historical record.

GOV-PART-001 · Mandatory · Top global rule

No administrative participant or transcript exclusion

Human rights and each participant’s right to be heard take precedence over analytical, administrative, technical, and operational convenience. Participant contributions are research source material. No AI output, model judgment, parsing result, validation rule, numbering issue, relationship error, persistence failure, database state, worker failure, or analytical label has authority to disqualify a participant, exclude a transcript, classify it as unusable, or permanently render it non-processible.

Required
Every transcript remains retained and processible or reprocessable. Formal incompleteness is recorded as not ready yet for complete-case analysis, never as participant exclusion. System and computational failures remain visible as system-owned attention states with a reason, a retry or reprocessing path, and escalation to researchers or system experts when unresolved.
Prohibited
“Unusable,” “legacy unusable,” “excluded,” or equivalent administrative dispositions based on model output, missing analytical objects, validation rejection, numbering, evidence-link failure, database failure, or any other computational result.
Responsibility
The platform and its operators bear system failure. A failure must never be silently transferred to a participant through an adverse label, hidden omission, loss of processibility, or removal from researcher attention.
Review evidence
Queue and disposition states, failure handling, database constraints and functions, dashboard actions and labels, transcript retention, and safe retry or reprocessing paths.

GOV-PART-002 · Mandatory · Researcher directive

No analytical validator

Stage 1 must not use an application, schema, prompt, model, or database validator to accept or reject Meaning Units, Codes, Categories, Themes, relationships, counts, summaries, reports, participants, or transcripts.

Required
Preserve the exact completed model response first. Relational display is a non-rejecting system projection. Anything the system cannot project remains in the preserved output with a visible system-owned note and a path for investigation.
Prohibited
Discarding model objects, accumulating invalid-reason lists, rejecting a whole report, or converting a projection or storage limitation into an adverse case status.
Responsibility
Researchers judge analytical usefulness. The platform and its experts diagnose and resolve technical problems.
Review evidence
Exact raw model output, processing notes, database projection, worker failure behavior, dashboard visibility, and withdrawn validator history.

GOV-PRIV-001 · Mandatory

Participant anonymity at all times

Participants must remain anonymous throughout recruitment, consent, interviewing, storage, analysis, review, and export.

Prohibited
Requiring or retaining a participant’s real-world identity when it is not necessary for the approved research purpose.
Review evidence
Participant flow, stored identifiers, exported fields, and any protected pseudonym crosswalk.

GOV-PRIV-002 · Mandatory

No identity workaround for RLS

Identity-based participant accounts or authentication must not be introduced merely to satisfy database or Row Level Security architecture.

Required
Database and application security must conform to the anonymous participation model, not redefine participants as identity-linked users.
Review evidence
Data-access diagram, grants and policy review, participant request path, and authentication inventory.

GOV-ACCESS-001 · Mandatory

Access protection must preserve anonymity

Participant interview data must be protected through least-privilege, session-scoped or server-mediated controls that do not reveal or require participant identity.

Prohibited
Broad client access, identity collection for convenience, or a security repair that breaks anonymous participation.
Review evidence
Documented read/write paths, authorization boundaries, secret handling, and access tests.

GOV-AI-001 · Mandatory

Researcher-only, explicit AI control

AI analysis may begin only through an explicit operation selected and started by an authorized researcher.

Prohibited
Automatic analysis caused by participant activity, page loading, data refresh, design loading, navigation, or another read action.
Review evidence
Researcher authorization boundary, visible start action, API contract, and execution audit event.

GOV-AI-002 · Mandatory

Exact model choice and confirmed plan

Before paid execution, the researcher must select the provider and exact model and confirm a visible execution plan describing the operation, scope, source set, and expected stages.

Prohibited
Silent provider or model substitution, ambiguous model families, or execution before plan confirmation.
Review evidence
Confirmed plan snapshot and the requested and resolved provider/model identifiers stored with the run.

GOV-AI-003 · Mandatory

No hidden paid processing

Viewing, filtering, sorting, downloading, refreshing, or otherwise reading existing data must not trigger analysis, translation, synthesis, model calls, or other paid processing.

Required
Read operations and paid computational operations must have separate, inspectable contracts.
Review evidence
Network trace, endpoint behavior, cost/event logs, and tests showing read-only actions remain read-only.

GOV-METHOD-001 · Mandatory

Fresh analysis starts from original sources

When an operation is defined as fresh independent analysis, the model must receive original source material, not prior AI analysis, labels, summaries, or conclusions.

Prohibited
Silent continuation, repair, seeding, or analytical anchoring from earlier AI output.
Review evidence
Recorded input manifest, source transcript IDs, prompt assembly, and exclusion of earlier analysis IDs.

GOV-METHOD-002 · Mandatory

Cross-case work is a separate stage

Cross-case analysis, refinement, grouping, or synthesis must be a separately researcher-triggered operation. It must never begin automatically after case-level analysis.

Required
A new scope, plan, confirmation, run identity, and provenance record for every cross-case stage.
Review evidence
Stage locks, separate start controls, distinct job records, and explicit source-case selection.

GOV-PROV-001 · Mandatory

Researcher-visible provenance

Every computational output must retain traceable lineage to its source transcript or records, operation, input scope, rule versions, prompt or instruction version, provider, exact model, timestamps, and analytical relationships.

Required
Provenance must be accessible to researchers and reviewers, not hidden as an internal implementation detail.
Review evidence
On-screen lineage, exportable identifiers, run metadata, source links, and version references.

GOV-OPS-001 · Mandatory

Active operations can be stopped

An authorized researcher must be able to stop active analysis, translation, synthesis, or other computational operations.

Required
The stop request, observed stop point, partial effects, and final status remain recorded rather than concealed.
Review evidence
Visible stop control, durable cancellation state, worker behavior, and retained audit events.

GOV-HIST-001 · Mandatory

Historical states remain distinguishable

Current, completed, stopped, failed, partial, legacy, superseded, and withdrawn outputs must remain visibly and technically distinguishable.

Prohibited
Overwriting source analyses, presenting legacy output as current, or hiding stopped and failed attempts.
Review evidence
Immutable identifiers, status labels, predecessor/successor lineage, and filters that do not collapse unlike states.

GOV-CHANGE-001 · Mandatory

Governance changes are traceable

Every change to these rules must create a new version and preserve the prior wording, effective date, reason, responsible decision-maker, affected rule IDs, and implementation impact.

Prohibited
Silent edits, deletion of earlier versions, or retroactively claiming that a newer rule governed an older operation.
Review evidence
Append-only change record, source-control history, review decision, and rule version stored with affected future operations.

Architecture boundary for this governance version

This governance-page change does not enable Row Level Security, create participant accounts, or introduce participant authentication. Any future access-control repair must be designed and tested separately against the anonymity requirements above. Security is mandatory; participant identity is not an acceptable shortcut for achieving it.

Change control and version history

Governance versions are append-only. A proposed change must be reviewed independently from ordinary dashboard activity and must identify its privacy, methodological, operational, and implementation consequences before it takes effect.

GRG-PPR-1.2.0 · Stage 1 validator withdrawn

  • Effective: September 1, 2026
  • Status: In force
  • Affected rule: GOV-PART-002

Withdrew the system-derived Stage-1 analytical validator and its whole-report rejection behavior. Requires exact model-output preservation, non-rejecting relational projection, and visible system-owned processing notes.

GRG-PPR-1.1.0 · Participant transcript non-exclusion

  • Effective: September 1, 2026
  • Status: Superseded by GRG-PPR-1.2.0
  • Affected rule: GOV-PART-001

Established the top global rule that no administrative, AI, analytical, computational, or database outcome may disqualify a participant, exclude a transcript, classify it as unusable, or permanently render it non-processible. Formal completion is a readiness state only.

GRG-PPR-1.0.0 · Initial standing specification

  • Effective: September 1, 2026
  • Status: Superseded by GRG-PPR-1.1.0
  • Affected rules: GOV-PRIV-001 through GOV-CHANGE-001

Established participant anonymity and privacy as hard constraints; separated platform governance from operational dashboards and configurable analysis rules; codified explicit researcher control, analytical independence, provenance, cancellation, historical integrity, and traceable change control.

Document class: independent global governance specification. The page has no analysis, translation, authentication, or database action. It is safe to inspect as a read-only policy document.
